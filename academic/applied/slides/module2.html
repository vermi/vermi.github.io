Threat Actor Attributes

📍 Internal vs External

Internal: Employees, contractors, insiders
External: Hackers, nation-states, criminals

🧠 Sophistication Level

Low: Script kiddies, unskilled attackers
High: APTs, nation-state actors

💰 Resources/Funding

Limited: Individual hackers, hobbyists
Extensive: Governments, organized crime

🎯 Intent/Motivation

Various: Money, politics, revenge
Factor: Determines targets and methods

Understanding these attributes helps predict attacker behavior and build appropriate defenses.

The "Hats" Hackers Wear

🤠 White Hat

Authorized attackers

Ethical hackers, penetration testers

Goal: Find and fix vulnerabilities

🎩 Black Hat

Unauthorized attackers

Malicious intent, personal gain

Goal: Exploit for profit or harm

🎭 Gray Hat

Semi-authorized attackers

Good intentions, no permission

Still illegal and unethical!

Unskilled Attackers ("Script Kiddies")

💡 Who They Are

  • Limited technical skills, use tools created by others.
  • Often young, working alone with few resources.
  • Target schools, universities, and easy opportunities.
  • Motivated by proving their skills or "because it's there".

⚠️ Why They're Still Dangerous

🛠️ Easy Tools Available

Free DDoS tools, virus creators, and ransomware-as-a-service are readily available online.

🎯 Quantity Over Quality

Many attackers are constantly scanning for any vulnerable target — if you're vulnerable, they will find you.

Hacktivists: Hacking for a Cause

🔥 Current Hacktivist Activity (2024)

  • Anonymous Sudan: DDoS attacks against governments worldwide - leaders indicted by U.S. DOJ in 2024.
  • Pro-Russian Groups: 96% of attacks targeting Europe, especially Ukraine and NATO countries.
  • #OpVenezuela: Anonymous attacked 45+ government websites after election disputes.
  • Gaza/Ukraine Conflicts: Massive increase in politically motivated cyberattacks.

⚡ 2024 Hacktivist Evolution

  • Ransomware Integration: Groups like Stormous and GhostSec now using ransomware for "destructive operations".
  • Telegram Coordination: Moving from dark web to encrypted messaging for easier recruitment.
  • Critical Infrastructure: 23% of attacks on operational technology systems now from hacktivists.
🎭 Famous Hacktivist: Anonymous

Decentralized collective behind attacks on Scientology, ISIS, governments. Recent operations include Iranian government sites and Chinese websites during COVID protests.

Nation-State Attackers: APTs

🚨 2024: The Year of Nation-State Cyber Warfare

China: 2.4 million daily attacks on Taiwan (doubled from 2023).
Russia: 4,315 cyberattacks on Ukraine (70% increase).
Salt Typhoon (China): Infiltrated 8+ U.S. telecom companies including AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile.

🇨🇳 China (Salt Typhoon): 2-year espionage campaign against U.S. telecoms. Stole customer call data and government surveillance requests.
🇷🇺 Russia (APT29/Midnight Blizzard): Hacked Microsoft corporate emails, targeting senior leadership and cybersecurity teams since Nov 2023.
🇮🇷 Iran (Peach Sandstorm): Over a decade of espionage using "Tickler" malware against space industry and critical infrastructure.
🇰🇵 North Korea (Lazarus Group): Financial cybercrime to fund weapons programs. Known for WannaCry and crypto thefts.

🔬 Zero-Day Attacks: APTs discover unknown vulnerabilities and exploit them before patches exist.

Insider Threats & Shadow IT

👤 Insider Threats

  • Who: Employees, contractors, vendors
  • Advantage: Already have access and knowledge
  • Motivations: Money, revenge, activism, ideology
  • Famous: Edward Snowden (NSA whistleblower)

☁️ Shadow IT

  • What: Unauthorized technology use by employees
  • Examples: Personal Dropbox, unapproved apps
  • Intent: Usually helpful, not malicious
  • Risk: Data outside organizational control

🕵️ Behavioral Assessment is Key: Work with HR to identify unusual behavior patterns. Shadow IT often indicates unmet business needs.

Why Do Attackers Attack?

💰 Financial Gain

Ransomware, fraud, theft

🕵️ Espionage

Steal secrets, intellectual property

🚫 Service Disruption

Take down critical systems

🗳️ Political Beliefs

Hacktivism, ideology

😤 Revenge

Get back at employers, rivals

🌪️ Chaos/Disruption

Some just want to watch the world burn

💡 Understanding motivation helps predict targets and build defenses!

🤔 Think-Pair-Share: Threat Actor Analysis

Scenario: Your University's Wi-Fi Network Gets Hacked

Someone breaks into your campus Wi-Fi, steals student login credentials, and posts a message saying "Your security is weak! Fix it before someone really bad gets in."

⏰ Activity (7 minutes)

Step 1: Think individually - What type of threat actor is this? (2 min)

Step 2: Pair up - Compare your analysis (3 min)

Step 3: Share with class - Let's discuss! (2 min)

🎯 Consider These Clues:

  • The target (university)
  • The message left behind
  • What they stole vs. what they could have done
  • The apparent motivation

Common Threat Vectors: How Attackers Get In

📧 Message-Based Attacks

Email: Phishing, malware attachments | SMS/Text: Smishing attacks | Social Media: Fake accounts, info gathering

🌐 Network-Based Attacks

Wireless: Unsecured Wi-Fi, Bluetooth | Wired: Physical access to network jacks | Open Ports: Unprotected services

💾 Device & Media Attacks

USB Drops: Malicious USB drives in parking lots | Files/Images: Hidden malware in documents | Default Credentials

🔗 Supply Chain Attacks

Software: Compromised updates and patches | Hardware: Backdoors inserted during manufacturing | MSPs: Managed service provider compromises

Building Your Threat Intelligence

🆓 Open Source Intelligence (OSINT)

  • Government: CISA, FBI alerts
  • Vendors: Microsoft, Cisco advisories
  • Community: MISP, Threat Exchange
  • Dark Web: Tor browser monitoring

💰 Commercial Intelligence

  • Proprietary: Custom research and analysis
  • Curated: Filtered and validated data
  • Service: Expert analysis included
  • Real-time: Faster threat updates

🎯 What Threat Feeds Include

Technical: IP addresses, domains, file hashes

Context: Who might target you and why

IoCs: Indicators of Compromise

TTPs: Tactics, Techniques, Procedures

🌟 Connect This to YOUR Life

  • Financial Crime: Your bank accounts, credit cards, identity.
  • School Networks: Target of unskilled attackers and sometimes hacktivists.
  • Your Data: Future career info valuable to nation-states and competitors.

Chapter Summary

🌍 Today's Threat Landscape

Cybersecurity threats have evolved from lone hobbyists to sophisticated nation-states, organized crime, and everything in between.

🎯 Know Your Enemy

Understanding threat actor attributes and motivations helps predict their targets and build appropriate defenses.

🚪 Multiple Attack Paths

Attackers use diverse threat vectors - email, networks, devices, and supply chains all need protection.

📊 Intelligence is Power

Building robust threat intelligence programs helps organizations stay ahead of evolving threats.

🚨 Bottom Line

The threat landscape is constantly evolving. Stay informed, stay vigilant, and always assume someone is trying to attack you!
