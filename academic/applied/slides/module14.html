What Is Security Architecture?

The blueprint that decides where controls live, how they work together, and why they support the business.

📐 Architecture

The big picture: zones, trust boundaries, data flows, and which models (cloud, on-prem, hybrid) the organization uses.

✏️ Design

The specific choices: which firewall goes where, how VPN access works, what gets encrypted and with what.

🔧 Implementation

Building and configuring it: rule sets, hardening baselines, backup jobs, and monitoring.

Why it matters: Security bolted on after the fact is expensive and full of gaps. Security designed in from the start is cheaper, simpler, and harder to bypass.

Secure Design Principles

🔑 Least Privilege

Every user, service, and system gets only the access it needs, and nothing more.

🧅 Defense in Depth

Layer controls so that when one fails, another still stands between the attacker and the asset.

🔒 Secure by Default

Ship and deploy with the safe settings turned on. Users should have to opt out of security, not opt in.

🧯 Fail Securely

Decide in advance what happens when a control breaks: stay open for availability or close for confidentiality.

👥 Separation of Duties

No single person or component can complete a sensitive action alone.

✂️ Minimize Attack Surface

Remove unused services, ports, accounts, and features. What isn't there can't be exploited.

Comparing Architecture Models

🏢 On-Premises

Full control of hardware, data location, and physical security. You also own every patch, every failure, and every upgrade.

☁️ Cloud (IaaS / PaaS / SaaS)

Fast scaling and built-in redundancy under a shared responsibility model. Misconfiguration is the leading risk.

🔀 Hybrid

Mixes on-prem and cloud. Flexible, but identity, logging, and policy must stay consistent across both sides.

📦 Virtualization & Containers

Efficient isolation on shared hardware. Watch for VM escape, image sprawl, and vulnerable base images.

⚡ Serverless & Microservices

Small, independent pieces with less server management, but many more APIs and permissions to secure.

🏭 IoT, ICS/SCADA & Embedded

Long lifespans, limited compute, and often no way to patch. Segment them and monitor closely.

Architecture Considerations

Every model is a trade-off. Architects weigh these factors against the organization's needs and risk tolerance.

Operations

  • Availability
  • Resilience
  • Responsiveness
  • Scalability

Management

  • Ease of deployment
  • Ease of recovery
  • Patch availability
  • Inability to patch

Business

  • Cost
  • Risk transference
  • Power
  • Compute

Exam Note: Moving to the cloud can transfer some risk to a provider, but it never transfers accountability. The organization still owns its data.

Designing Enterprise Infrastructure

🗺️ Security Zones

Group systems by trust level (internet, screened subnet/DMZ, internal, restricted) and control every crossing between zones.

📍 Device Placement

Put controls at the boundaries they protect: firewalls between zones, sensors where traffic concentrates, WAFs in front of web apps.

🚦 Failure Modes

  • Fail-open: traffic flows if the device dies (favors availability)
  • Fail-closed: traffic stops if the device dies (favors security)

🔌 Active vs. Passive

  • Inline / active: sits in the traffic path and can block (IPS)
  • Tap / monitor / passive: sees a copy of traffic and alerts (IDS)

Infrastructure Building Blocks

Network Appliances

  • Jump server: a hardened entry point for admin access
  • Proxy: brokers and filters requests
  • IDS / IPS: detect or block malicious traffic
  • Load balancer: spreads work across servers
  • Sensors: feed data to monitoring and SIEM

Firewalls & Secure Connections

  • WAF: protects web applications
  • UTM / NGFW: many security functions in one device
  • VPN, TLS, IPSec: encrypted tunnels for remote access
  • SD-WAN: manages wide-area links in software
  • SASE: networking and security delivered from the cloud

Connect the chapters: You configured several of these in Module 12's firewall and RADIUS labs. Architecture is deciding where each one belongs.

Zero Trust Architecture

"Never trust, always verify."

🧠 Control Plane

Makes the access decisions.

  • Adaptive identity
  • Threat scope reduction
  • Policy-driven access control
  • Policy engine & policy administrator

📨 Data Plane

Carries out the decisions.

  • Implicit trust zones
  • Subject / system
  • Policy enforcement point (PEP)

Being "inside the network" earns nothing. Each request is judged on identity, device health, and context, every time.

Protecting Data by Design

💾 At Rest

Stored on disks, databases, backups. Use full-disk and database encryption.

📡 In Transit

Moving across networks. Use TLS, IPSec, and VPNs.

🧮 In Use

Loaded in memory and being processed. The hardest state to protect.

Protection Methods

  • Encryption and hashing
  • Masking and tokenization
  • Obfuscation
  • Segmentation and permission restrictions

Design Questions

  • How is the data classified?
  • Where may it legally be stored (sovereignty, geolocation)?
  • Who needs it, and in what form?

Designing for Resilience

⚙️ High Availability

  • Load balancing: many servers share the work
  • Clustering: nodes take over for each other
  • Platform diversity & multi-cloud: one flaw or provider can't take everything down

🌎 Site Considerations

  • Hot site: ready to run almost immediately
  • Warm site: hardware ready, data needs restoring
  • Cold site: space and power only
  • Geographic dispersion: one disaster can't hit every site

🗄️ Backups

  • Onsite and offsite copies
  • Frequency matched to acceptable data loss
  • Encryption, snapshots, replication, journaling

🔋 Power & Continuity

  • UPS for short outages, generators for long ones
  • Continuity of operations planning (COOP)
  • Capacity planning for people, technology, and infrastructure

A Plan You Haven't Tested Is a Guess

Resilience testing, from least to most disruptive:

1. Tabletop Exercise

The team talks through a scenario around a table. No systems are touched.

2. Simulation

A realistic practice run of the response, without affecting production.

3. Parallel Processing

The backup systems run alongside production to confirm they produce the same results.

4. Failover

Production actually switches to the backup. The most realistic test, and the riskiest.

Endpoints and Wireless in the Architecture

💻 Endpoints (Module 11)

  • Hardening baselines and security templates
  • EDR / XDR for detection and response
  • Host firewalls and least-privilege permissions
  • Asset inventory: you can't protect what you don't know about

📶 Wireless & Mobile (Module 13)

  • WPA3 and 802.1X / RADIUS authentication
  • Guest Wi-Fi in its own isolated zone
  • Site surveys and heat maps for access point placement
  • MDM with a clear deployment model (BYOD, COPE, CYOD)

Architect's view: Endpoints and wireless clients are where users meet the network, which makes them the most common first foothold for attackers.

Case Study: A Small Medical Clinic

A 30-person clinic stores patient records, takes card payments at the front desk, offers guest Wi-Fi in the waiting room, and lets doctors check schedules from their phones. Today everything shares one flat network.

Requirement Design Decision Principle
Keep patient records confidential Restricted zone, encryption at rest, role-based access Least privilege
Protect card payments Separate payment VLAN, firewall rules between zones Segmentation
Guests need internet Isolated guest SSID with internet-only access Minimize attack surface
Doctors use phones MDM enrollment, conditional access to the scheduling app Zero Trust
Records must survive ransomware Encrypted offsite backups, regularly test-restored Resilience

Key Takeaways

🏛️ Architecture comes first. Good design makes every later control cheaper and more effective.

⚖️ Every model is a trade-off. Cloud, on-prem, and hybrid each shift cost, control, and responsibility.

🗺️ Zones and placement matter. Control the boundaries, and decide how devices should fail.

🔐 Protect data in every state with the right mix of encryption, masking, tokenization, and access control.

🛟 Assume failure. Build redundancy, keep tested backups, and practice recovery before you need it.
