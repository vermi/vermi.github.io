The 2024–2025 Landscape

No Federal Privacy Law

Congress's continued failure to pass a comprehensive privacy law left states and federal agencies to lead the charge in defining and regulating cybersecurity and privacy.

State Expansion

  • 7 states enacted new comprehensive privacy laws in 2024
  • 4 states' laws took effect in 2024 (FL, TX, OR, MT)
  • By 2026, 20 states (~50% of U.S. population) will have comprehensive privacy laws

The Trump administration signals a significant federal shift: deregulation, pro-innovation, and reversal of Biden-era policies around content moderation, AI, and digital assets.

PART I

State Privacy Laws

Comprehensive laws, children's data, biometrics, and emerging frameworks

State Privacy Law Expansion

New Laws Passed in 2024

Minnesota, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Maryland, Kentucky, Rhode Island

Laws Taking Effect in 2025

Delaware, Iowa, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Tennessee, Maryland, Minnesota

Early 2026

Indiana, Kentucky, Rhode Island

16 additional states are actively considering data privacy legislation: AL, AR, GA, HI, IL, MA, MS, NM, NY, OH, OK, PA, SC, VT, WA, WV.

Core Elements of State Privacy Laws

Common Rights

  • Access personal data
  • Correct personal data
  • Delete personal data
  • Opt out of targeted advertising
  • Opt out of profiling
  • Opt out of data sales

Key Variations

  • Maryland: Strictest—bans sale of sensitive data entirely
  • Minnesota: First "right to question" automated decisions
  • Iowa: Most controller-friendly—opt-out for sensitive data
  • Tennessee: NIST-based affirmative defense

State Spotlight: Maryland

Strictest in the Nation

Maryland's Online Data Privacy Act (effective Oct 2025) is the only state to prohibit the sale of sensitive personal information entirely.

Minors' Protections

Prohibits sale of minors' personal information and processing for targeted advertising. Defines "minor" as anyone under 18 (vs. under 16 in CA and VA).

Broader Knowledge Standard

Applies to businesses that "knew or should have known" the consumer's age—broader than the "actual knowledge" standard used in Texas and Connecticut.

State Spotlight: Minnesota's "Right to Question"

First of Its Kind

Minnesota Consumer Data Privacy Act is the first state to offer consumers the right to question automated decisions.

The Right to Question Includes

  • Know the reason behind the decision
  • Know what actions could secure a different outcome
  • Review the personal data used
  • Correct inaccurate data and request reevaluation

As businesses adopt automated decisionmaking tools, this creates a unique compliance area for companies operating in Minnesota.

Key Divergences: Automation & "Sale"

Automated Decisionmaking Opt-Out

  • "Solely" automated: CT, DE, FL, IN, MD, MT, NE, NH, RI, TN, TX
  • Broader scope: CA, CO, KY, MN, NJ, OR, VA
  • No opt-out: IA, UT

Definition of "Sale"

  • Monetary or other valuable consideration: CA, CO, CT, DE, FL, MD, MN, MT, NE, NH, NJ, OR, RI, TX
  • Monetary consideration only: IN, IA, KY, TN, UT, VA

These differences have major compliance implications for companies that exchange data for services rather than monetary payment.

Children's Online Privacy: State Approaches

State Minors Laws

  • Florida: Bans social media for children ≤13; parental consent for 14–15
  • Georgia: Restricts social media for under-16 without parental consent
  • Maryland Kids Code: Impact assessments for children's data; default high privacy
  • NY SAFE Act: Bans "addictive feeds" for minors without consent
  • California: "Addictive feeds" ban—blocked by Ninth Circuit

Age Thresholds for Opt-In Consent

  • Under 16: CA, CT, MT, NH, OR (opt-in consent for sale/ads)
  • Under 17: MN, NJ
  • Under 18: DE, MD (strictest—no targeted ads/sale)

Biometric Data Legislation

Illinois BIPA Amendments (Aug 2024)

Same biometric data + same method = single violation. Aims to prevent exponential damages from Cothron v. White Castle ruling.

Colorado Biometric Protections (July 2025)

Retention schedules, incident response protocols, deletion requirements, and employee consent rules for biometric identifiers.

Neural & Biological Data

Colorado and California expanded "sensitive data" to include neural data—information from measuring nervous system activity. Colorado goes further to include "biological data."

PART II

Federal Legislation

Comprehensive privacy bills, PADFAA, and congressional action

Federal Privacy Legislation: APRA & PADFAA

APRA — Stalled

  • American Privacy Rights Act introduced Apr 2024
  • Bipartisan, bicameral effort
  • Would grant access, correction, deletion, export rights
  • Would preempt most state privacy laws
  • Opposition from state regulators (CPPA), civil rights groups
  • Markup cancelled June 2024—no further action

PADFAA — Enacted

  • Protecting Americans' Data from Foreign Adversaries Act
  • Signed into law April 2024; effective June 2024
  • Prohibits data brokers from transferring sensitive data to China, Russia, Iran, North Korea
  • Broadly defines "personally identifiable sensitive data"

Key divides remain: preemption of state laws (Republican position) vs. private right of action (Democratic position).

Other Federal Bills & Cybersecurity Legislation

Children's Safety

  • Kids Online Safety Act: Duty of care for platforms, protective defaults
  • COPPA 2.0: Extends protections to teens 12–17, bans targeted ads
  • Both passed Senate; no House action yet

Cybersecurity Bills

  • Healthcare Cybersecurity Act: CISA + HHS collaboration
  • Farm & Food Cybersecurity Act: Food-related cyber exercises
  • Health Infrastructure Security Act: Mandatory minimum cyber standards

Other Proposed Bills

Stop Spying Bosses Act • No Robot Bosses Act • Reproductive Data Privacy Act • Verifying Kids' Online Privacy Act • American Donor Privacy Act

PART III

Federal Enforcement & Guidance

FTC, CFPB, SEC, HHS, DOJ, and other agency actions

Federal Trade Commission: Leadership Change

New Chair Andrew Ferguson (Jan 2025)

Replaced Lina Khan. His agenda: "Reverse Khan's anti-business agenda," hold Big Tech accountable, protect free speech, and "no more novel and legally dubious consumer protection cases."

White House Oversight

Trump executive order requires FTC (and SEC, FCC) to consult White House on strategic plans, priorities, and draft regulations.

Republican Majority Coming

Mark Meador nominated as new Republican commissioner. If confirmed, FTC will have a Republican majority for the first time since 2022.

FTC: AI, Surveillance & Data Security

AI & Algorithmic Bias

  • Settled with convenience store chain over AI facial recognition (5-year ban)
  • Action against IntelliVision for unsubstantiated FRT accuracy claims
  • Chair Ferguson opposes "pro-regulation side of AI debate"

Surveillance Pricing

  • Orders to 8 companies offering surveillance pricing products
  • Study reveals how consumer data affects pricing
  • Public comments opened then shut down by Chair Ferguson
  • Ferguson views targeted advertising as "beneficial to consumers"

FTC: Notable 2024 Enforcement Actions

Corporate Landlord: $45M settlement—junk fees, data retention violations, consumer refunds.

Digital Marketing / Data Aggregator: Banned from sharing precise location data; must destroy stored data.

Substance Abuse Telehealth: Banned from sharing health data for advertising; required privacy programs.

Online Therapy Provider: Shared sensitive data (emails, IPs, health answers) with advertisers without consent.

Data Brokers: Tracking and selling sensitive location data—banned from collecting/selling.

Security Camera Company: $2.95M penalty—CAN-SPAM violation, hackers accessed hospital cameras.

Key trend: FTC requiring deletion of improperly collected data and implementing comprehensive privacy programs.

FTC: COPPA Updates & Children's Privacy

COPPA Amendments Finalized (Jan 16, 2025)

Shifts burden from parents to service providers.

Key Changes

  • Opt-in parental consent required for sharing children's data with third parties for targeted advertising
  • Data retention limits—only as long as "reasonably necessary"
  • Public disclosure requirements for Safe Harbor programs
  • "Personal information" expanded to include biometric and government-issued identifiers

FTC denied ESRB's facial age estimation application for COPPA consent verification—insufficient information.

CFPB: A Dramatic Shift

Effectively Frozen

Director Rohit Chopra removed January 31, 2025. Acting Directors froze all enforcement, litigation, and rulemaking.

Operational Shutdown

  • DC office closed; Federal Reserve funding cancelled
  • $100M+ in vendor contracts cancelled
  • Probationary staff fired; enforcement actions dismissed
  • Key 2024 rules suspended: data broker FCRA rule, Section 1033 open banking rule, AI housing appraisal rule

Gap filling: FTC, state AGs, and private litigants expected to increase activity as CFPB enforcement wanes.

SEC: Cybersecurity Disclosure & Enforcement

Cyber Disclosure Rule (2024)

  • ~50 companies filed cybersecurity disclosures on Form 8-K
  • Fewer than 20% reported material impact
  • 5 new compliance interpretations—4 on ransomware payments
  • New Reg S-P: 30-day breach notification for broker-dealers and advisers

Key Enforcement

  • Software company case largely dismissed—court rejected cybersecurity-as-accounting-controls theory
  • Transfer agent fined $850K for failing to secure client funds
  • Stock exchange operator: $10M settlement
  • Marketing firm: $2.1M for inadequate cyber disclosure controls

2025 outlook: Pro-business SEC Chair expected; Republican commissioners skeptical of cyber enforcement; but new Cyber and Emerging Technologies Unit formed.

HHS & HIPAA: Cybersecurity in Healthcare

Risk Analysis Initiative (Oct 2024)

Guides healthcare organizations in evaluating cybersecurity practices for electronic protected health information (ePHI).

Proposed HIPAA Security Rule Update (Dec 2024)

Encrypt ePHI, deploy malware controls, require multi-factor authentication. Now frozen under Trump's "Regulatory Freeze Pending Review."

Notable Enforcement

  • Clearway Pain Solutions: $1.19M penalty for ePHI breach (34,000+ individuals)
  • Holy Redeemer Family Medicine: Settlement for disclosing full medical record to employer
  • Bryan County Ambulance: $90K—first enforcement under Risk Analysis Initiative

DOJ & Other Federal Agencies

Department of Justice

  • Final Rule restricting foreign adversaries' data access (China, Cuba, Iran, NK, Russia, Venezuela)
  • Lawsuit against social media company for COPPA violations
  • Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative settlements
  • Dark web takedowns: Nulled, Cracked, Rydox, Incognito Market
  • LockBit disruption: 2,000+ victims, $120M+ in ransoms

Other Agencies

  • Commerce/NIST: CSF 2.0 with new "Govern" function; Kaspersky ban
  • DHS/CISA: US-EU incident reporting harmonization
  • DOD: CMMC program finalized for defense contractors
  • DOE: Cybersecurity baselines for energy; ETAC operational
  • FCC: ~$395M in fines/settlements; Cyber Trust Mark

FCC: Enforcement & Rulemaking

Major Fines & Settlements

  • Wireless carriers: ~$200M for sharing customer location data without consent
  • Phone captioning company: $34.6M for unlawful call content retention
  • Prepaid service provider: $16M for failing to protect customer info
  • Major carrier (data breach): $13M—failed to ensure vendor destroyed data
  • Major carrier (multiple breaches): $31.5M—exposed 47.8M records including SSNs

Cyber Trust Mark (March 2024): Voluntary cybersecurity labeling program for IoT devices meeting privacy/security standards—smart appliances, fitness trackers, etc.

State Agency Enforcement

Texas AG — Most Active

  • Launched dedicated data privacy team (June 2024)
  • Investigating car manufacturers' data collection
  • Sued car manufacturer under Deceptive Trade Practices Act
  • 100+ notices for data broker registration failures
  • Filed SCOPE Act complaint against social media platform

Other States

  • California CPPA: Data broker sweep; $6,600 registration fees; ADMT regulations
  • CA AG: Streaming service sweep; $375K tech company settlement
  • Connecticut: Warning letters, 30 complaints in first 6 months
  • Colorado: Recognized Global Privacy Control as universal opt-out
  • Oregon & Virginia: Initiated confidential investigations

State AGs are expected to be particularly active in 2025 as federal enforcement pulls back under the Trump administration.

PART IV

Civil Litigation

Data breach, wiretapping, BIPA, TCPA, CCPA, and emerging trends

Data Breach Litigation

2024 By the Numbers

2,850 data breaches (down from 3,122 in 2023) but victims increased 257% due to the scale of major breaches.

Major Settlements

  • $65M: Health network—ransomware, 135K patients, medical photos leaked
  • $30M: Personal genomics company—6M+ customers' data stolen
  • $15M: Mobile payment company—two breaches, 8.2M users exposed

Key Legal Developments

  • Standing: Risk of future harm sufficient if actual attempted misuse alleged
  • Fiduciary duty: Split—employer-employee (rejected) vs. healthcare provider (accepted)
  • Telecom class actions consolidated in N.D. Texas—73M accounts exposed

Wiretapping & Online Tracking Litigation

The Flood of Cases

Hundreds of cases filed in 2024. Plaintiffs allege web tracking tools (pixels, SDKs, chat features) violate federal and state wiretap laws.

Damages Exposure

  • Federal: $100/day or $10K minimum
  • California: $5,000 per violation (all-party consent state)

Mixed Results

  • Win for defense: Keystrokes immediately "hashed" = no reading of "contents"
  • Win for defense: Social media SDKs—no evidence of intercepted "contents"
  • Loss for defense: Period-tracking app data shared via SDK—allowed to proceed
  • Major settlement: Tech company settled Incognito Mode tracking case—must delete billions of records

BIPA: Biometric Information Privacy Litigation

Defense-Friendly

  • 9th Circuit: Data must be "capable of identifying" the plaintiff
  • Damages Amendment: Same data + same method = single violation
  • Control matters: Designing software ≠ collecting data
  • AI training: Must plead photos were actually in training dataset

Plaintiff-Friendly

  • Cloud providers can be liable for third-party publishers' biometric use
  • Non-IL residents may bring BIPA claims if data processed within Illinois
  • Clearview AI: Preliminary settlement—23% equity stake (~$52M)
  • Texas CUBI: $1.4B settlement with social media company

CCPA & TCPA Litigation Trends

CCPA Private Right of Action

  • Statutory damages: $100–$750 per consumer per incident
  • Defendant must qualify as a "business" that determines how data is processed
  • Scope expanding: disclosure without consent now actionable (not just traditional breaches)

TCPA

  • Focus shifted from ATDS definition to other provisions post-Duguid
  • Free webinar fax = "unsolicited advertisement" (4th Circuit)
  • One-to-one consent rule went into effect Jan 2025—then vacated by 11th Circuit
  • Supreme Court to rule on FCC's interpretive authority under Hobbs Act

CFAA & Other Noteworthy Litigation

Computer Fraud & Abuse Act

  • Screen scraping case: Jury awarded $5K—then reversed (airline failed to prove $5K loss threshold)
  • IT admin: Access as account manager was authorized; lacked notice it wasn't
  • Former employees expanding access: Survived dismissal

Other Cases

  • Daniel's Law (NJ): Constitutional—privacy statute survives First Amendment challenge
  • Cellular data as property: 9th Circuit—data qualifies for conversion claims
  • VPPA: Split on tracking technology disclosures
  • NYC Biometric Law: "Profiting" limited to transactions involving the data itself

The Trump Administration's Impact

Key Changes

  • FTC: New Chair Ferguson—"no more legally dubious consumer protection cases"
  • CFPB: Effectively frozen—enforcement halted, rules suspended, staff cut
  • SEC: Pro-business Chair expected; reduced cyber enforcement
  • HHS: HIPAA Security Rule update frozen under "Regulatory Freeze Pending Review"
  • Executive Order: Independent agencies must consult White House on strategic plans

The priorities—deregulation, pro-innovation, reversal of Biden-era policies—are reshaping the federal landscape. State AGs and private litigants are expected to fill the enforcement gap.

2025 Outlook & Key Takeaways

Expect Continued

  • State privacy law expansion and enforcement
  • Focus on children's data and biometric privacy
  • Active civil litigation (breach, wiretapping, BIPA)
  • State AG enforcement filling federal gaps
  • DOJ cybercrime prosecution

Expect Shifts

  • Reduced FTC enforcement aggressiveness
  • CFPB effectively dormant or eliminated
  • SEC pulling back on cyber enforcement
  • Pro-innovation AI and technology policy at federal level
  • No comprehensive federal privacy law in near term

Bottom line: The compliance landscape is fragmenting—more state laws, less federal guidance, increased litigation risk.
