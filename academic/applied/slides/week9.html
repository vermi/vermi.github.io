Resilience & High Availability

Resilience is the ability to withstand disruptions. High Availability is the practice of ensuring systems remain operational, often through redundancy.

Load Balancing

Distributes incoming network traffic across multiple servers to ensure no single server becomes a bottleneck. Improves responsiveness and availability.

Clustering

A group of servers that work together as a single system. If one server (node) fails, another node in the cluster takes over its workload (failover).

Geographic Dispersion

Placing redundant systems or datacenters in different physical locations to protect against regional disasters like earthquakes or hurricanes.

Power Resiliency

Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS)

A large battery that provides instantaneous, short-term power in the event of an outage. Its purpose is to give systems enough time to either shut down gracefully or for a generator to start up.

Generator

A fuel-powered engine that provides long-term power during an extended outage. There is a short delay between the power loss and the generator starting, which is why a UPS is essential.

🚨 Real-World Example: OVHcloud Datacenter Fire (2021)

A massive fire completely destroyed one of OVHcloud's datacenters in France and damaged another, taking millions of websites offline. The investigation revealed that the datacenter lacked an automatic fire suppression system and had a wooden ceiling. This incident highlighted the critical importance of both power resiliency and physical safety systems, as customers who had not paid for backups to a separate geographic location lost their data permanently.

Backup Strategies

Full Backup

A complete copy of all data. It is the slowest to create but the fastest to restore.

Differential Backup

Copies all files that have changed since the last full backup. Restoration requires the full backup plus the latest differential.

Incremental Backup

Copies all files that have changed since the last backup of any kind. It is the fastest to create but the slowest to restore, as it requires the last full backup and all subsequent incrementals.

Snapshots

Captures the full state of a system (usually a virtual machine) at a specific point in time, allowing for quick rollbacks.

On-site vs. Off-site

On-site backups are fast, but off-site backups (in a different geographic location) are essential to protect against a site-wide disaster like a fire or flood.

Disaster Recovery Sites

🔥 Hot Site

A fully operational duplicate of the primary site with all hardware, software, and real-time data replication. Recovery is nearly instantaneous.

☀️ Warm Site

Has hardware and connectivity but relies on recent backups for data. Recovery takes hours to days.

❄️ Cold Site

Just a room with power and cooling. All hardware and data must be brought in. Recovery can take weeks.

Physical Security: The Perimeter

Fencing

The first line of defense. Height and material are key factors in its effectiveness as a deterrent and barrier.

Bollards

Short, sturdy posts used to prevent vehicles from ramming into buildings or accessing pedestrian areas.

Lighting

Illuminates critical areas to deter intruders and assist surveillance. Eliminating shadows is a primary goal.

Video Surveillance (CCTV)

Cameras used to monitor areas in real-time and record footage for later investigation. Acts as both a detective and a deterrent control.

Physical Security: Access Control

Access Control Vestibule (Mantrap)

A set of two interlocking doors where only one can be open at a time. Prevents tailgating/piggybacking.

Access Badges

Cards (using magnetic stripes or RFID) that grant access through readers. They also serve as visual identification.

Security Guards

Provide a human element for decision-making, observation, and response that automated systems cannot.

Sensors

Devices that detect changes in the environment, such as motion (infrared, microwave), pressure, or noise.

Chapter Summary

Resilience is Built on Redundancy

High availability is achieved by eliminating single points of failure through load balancers, clusters, redundant power (UPS/generators), and RAID.

Plan for Disaster

A robust backup strategy (full, differential, incremental) and a suitable disaster recovery site (hot, warm, or cold) are essential for business continuity.

Physical Security is the Foundation

Technical controls are meaningless if an attacker can simply walk in and steal a server. A layered defense using fences, access controls, and surveillance is critical.

🚨 Bottom Line

Availability is not an accident. It is the result of careful planning, redundant design, and a strong physical security posture.
