Cybersecurity Mini-Lab

What is OSINT?

OSINT stands for Open-Source Intelligence.

It's the practice of collecting and analyzing information from publicly available sources to create actionable intelligence. It's not hacking; it's advanced searching and analysis.

🕵️‍♂️

Why Does It Matter?

The "Good Guys"

Cybersecurity defenders use OSINT to find their own company's weaknesses before attackers do. Journalists use it for investigations. Researchers use it to gather data.

The "Bad Guys"

Attackers use OSINT for reconnaissance—the first step in any attack. They look for employee names, software versions, and other clues to plan their approach.

The OSINT Mindset

It's more than just searching. It's about...

  • ✓ Asking the right questions.
  • ✓ Being curious and persistent.
  • ✓ Connecting seemingly unrelated dots.
  • ✓ Following a trail of digital breadcrumbs.

Stage 1: Identify the Target

Analysts often begin by identifying what's most popular. Your first task is to find a specific webpage that ranks software development tools (IDEs) based on how often their download pages are searched.

Your Flag: According to this index, what is the #2 most popular IDE?

Stage 2: Find the Person

Now that you have identified the target IDE, your next task is to locate its open source project and find the person managing its monthly development sprints.

Clue: The author of the "Iteration Plan" is the likely suspect.

Your Flag: What is the full name (not username) of the current sprint planner?

Stage 3: Find the Contact

Professional email addresses are often listed in public records, especially for technical standards related to a project.

Clue: You may need to look for technical registrations or standards documents related to the project to find an official point of contact.

Your Flag: Find the sprint planner's publicly listed work email address.
