Course Description

This course builds upon the foundation established in Applied Cybersecurity I, focusing on advanced security operations, incident response, and security program management. Students will examine security architecture, resilience and recovery strategies, identity and access management, and security automation as outlined in CompTIA Security+ certification objectives. The course combines theoretical instruction with hands-on laboratory experiences where students will practice implementing identity and access management solutions, performing incident response activities, analyzing security data, and conducting security assessments in controlled environments. Lab activities include security incident simulations, digital forensics tasks, security automation configurations, and penetration testing fundamentals. This integrated approach prepares students for security roles by emphasizing practical applications of security governance, risk management, and compliance requirements in enterprise environments and completes preparation for the CompTIA Security+ certification exam.

Course Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

  1. Implement and maintain identity and access management solutions.
  2. Configure and monitor security alerting systems.
  3. Execute appropriate incident response procedures.
  4. Apply security automation and orchestration techniques.
  5. Demonstrate understanding of security governance and risk management.
  6. Conduct basic security assessments and audits.
  7. Prepare for the CompTIA Security+ certification exam.

Tentative Course Schedule

Week 1: Identity and Access Management I

Lecture Topics: Identity concepts and terminology, Authentication methods and multifactor authentication, Single sign-on and federation (LDAP, SAML, OAuth, OpenID), Account types and provisioning.

Lab Activities: ACI Skill Labs: Implement Identity & Access Management, LEXX: Creating a Domain User, LEXX: Creating Linux Users and Groups, Group Activity: IAM Scenario - New Employee Onboarding.

Reading Assignment: Security+ Study Guide: Chapter 8 - Identity and Access Management, CompTIA SY0-701 Objectives: Section 4.6 (Given a scenario, implement and maintain identity and access management).

Week 2: Identity and Access Management II

Lecture Topics: Access control schemes (RBAC, ABAC, MAC, DAC), Directory services and Kerberos authentication, Privileged access management, Permission creep and periodic access reviews.

Lab Activities: LEXX: Examining Kerberos Settings, LEXX: Examining Active Directory Objects, LEXX: Delegating Control in Active Directory.

Reading Assignment: Security+ Study Guide: Chapter 8 - Identity and Access Management (continued), CompTIA SY0-701 Objectives: Section 4.6.

Week 3: Monitoring and Incident Response

Lecture Topics: Security monitoring concepts and tools, Log analysis and security information and event management, Incident response lifecycle, Alerting, escalation, and threat hunting.

Lab Activities: ACI Skill Labs: Monitoring Computing Resources, LEXX: Viewing Windows Event Logs, LEXX: Viewing Linux Event Logs.

Reading Assignment: Security+ Study Guide: Chapter 14 - Monitoring and Incident Response, CompTIA SY0-701 Objectives: Section 4.4 (Explain security alerting and monitoring concepts and tools), CompTIA SY0-701 Objectives: Section 4.8 (Explain appropriate incident response activities).

Week 4: Digital Forensics

Lecture Topics: Digital forensics principles and procedures, Evidence collection and handling, Chain of custody, Forensic tools and techniques.

Lab Activities: ACI Skill Labs: Investigative Data Sources, Group Activity: Mini Tabletop Exercise (ransomware incident scenario).

Reading Assignment: Security+ Study Guide: Chapter 15 - Digital Forensics, CompTIA SY0-701 Objectives: Section 4.9 (Given a scenario, use data sources to support an investigation).

Week 5: Security Governance and Compliance

Lecture Topics: Security governance frameworks, Policies, standards, and procedures, Regulatory and legal compliance requirements, Security awareness practices.

Lab Activities: Discussion-based module; no vendor lab this week.

Reading Assignment: Security+ Study Guide: Chapter 16 - Security Governance and Compliance, CompTIA SY0-701 Objectives: Sections 5.1, 5.4, and 5.6.

📋 Project Milestone: Security Project Phase 1 Begins

Week 6: Risk Management and Privacy

Lecture Topics: Risk management process and concepts, Quantitative and qualitative risk analysis, Risk response strategies, Privacy principles, roles, and regulations.

Lab Activities: Activity: Risk Calculation Practice (SLE, ARO, and ALE scenarios), Activity: Identify the Strategy (risk management strategies), Activity: Name That Role (data privacy roles).

Reading Assignment: Security+ Study Guide: Chapter 17 - Risk Management and Privacy, CompTIA SY0-701 Objectives: Sections 5.2 and 5.3.

Week 7: Social Engineering and the Psychology of Security

Lecture Topics: Social engineering techniques and pretexting, Psychological principles of influence, Case studies: Kevin Mitnick and Motorola, DEF CON SECTF, Guest Lecture: Psychology of security (tentative).

Lab Activities: Case study analysis, OSINT self-assessment exercise.

Reading Assignment: Review Security+ Study Guide: Chapter 4 - Social Engineering and Password Attacks, CompTIA SY0-701 Objectives: Section 2.2.

📋 Project Milestone: Security Project Phase 1 Due

Generative AI and Technology Tools Policy

This course embraces the use of generative Al (such as ChatGPT, Claude, etc.) as valuable productivity and creativity tools. Students are encouraged to explore these technologies as part of their learning experience. To ensure these tools enhance rather than replace learning:

  • Encouraged Uses: Use Al tools to help understand difficult concepts, brainstorm ideas, improve your writing, or create initial drafts that you then refine.
  • Prohibited Uses: Do not use Al to answer exam questions or submit Al-generated assignments without substantial personal input and understanding.
  • Attribution Requirement: When Al tools play a significant role in your work, note this in your submission.

Using Al in ways that bypass the learning process (such as for exam questions or submitting work that isn't primarily yours) is a violation of academic integrity and will affect your course grade, which could result in course failure. Repeated violations will result in course failure.

The security tools and techniques taught in this class are for educational and defensive purposes only. Using these skills maliciously, either in or out of school, violates our professional ethics and will be referred for disciplinary action as appropriate.
